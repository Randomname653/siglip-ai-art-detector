# Results

All numbers are generated by `ergebnisse_md.py` from the result files of the runs; nothing is typed by hand.

**Protocol.** Every head is an ensemble of small MLPs on frozen image features. Test images were never in training (other creators/artists; the Telegram channels never at all). "Equal false-positive rate": each head gets the threshold at which it flags 1 % of human test art on average over three groups (Danbooru anime 2026, Wallhaven general <2022, Danbooru non-anime 2022-26). AUC needs no threshold.

## 7. Controls (p8sx)

**Scale intervention** (resampling leak test): human images upscaled shift by -0.48 logits, AI images downscaled by -1.45, at a class distance of about 34. A resampling leak would push humans up; they stay put.

## 13. Training with everyday edits (p9sx)

Texts and fonts differ from the robustness test; the 4x CDC upscaler was never used in training. Same data, same recipe, two seed runs (10 heads).

Edit augmentation makes the head a better separator under every edit (AUC up in all 13 rows, most for crops, greyscale and noise) and raises the hit rate on edited AI images, but it does not pull edited human images back below the 1 % threshold: the false-positive rate on crops, captions and upscaled humans stays at 2-5 %. On unedited data it is a tie. The 50 % centre crop is effectively a 256 px image upscaled to 512 — its false-positive rate matches the 256 px row of section 9, so it measures the small-image effect rather than cropping as such. Combining both ensembles (20 heads) lands between the two. The gains are real but small, so the production scanner stays on p8sx; p9sx is the candidate if edited or heavily recompressed AI images become a problem.
